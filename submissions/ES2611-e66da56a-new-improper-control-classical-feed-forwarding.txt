SUBMISSION TYPE: Hardware

NAME: Improper Control of Classical Feed-Forward Enabling a Covert Channel on a Quantum Computer

DESCRIPTION:

The product supports mid-circuit measurement and classical feed-forward
(conditional operations) on a quantum computer without policy controls on
their use, enabling a covert channel between quantum registers that share
no two-qubit gate or, on shared hardware, between tenants.

Dynamic-circuit features let a classical outcome measured mid-circuit
control later quantum operations. This is legitimate and expected within a
single job. The weakness is the absence of controls governing feed-forward:
an actor can measure a sender register and conditionally drive a receiver
register that is otherwise unconnected, encoding information into the
receiver's state. On a shared processor the same primitive can bridge
tenants. Because feed-forward is ordinary functionality, nothing in a
conventional circuit check flags its use as a channel, so the transmission
is covert.

Example: A sender register is measured mid-circuit; based on the measured
bit, a controlled operation is conditionally applied to a receiver register
that shares no entangling gate with the sender. Decoding the receiver
recovers the transmitted bit, establishing a channel between two registers
that appear independent. On a shared processor the same primitive can
bridge tenants. Consequences are primarily confidentiality (covert
transmission of information across registers or tenants) and integrity
(policy bypass). The weakness is introduced at architecture and design time
when dynamic-circuit features (mid-circuit measurement, classical
feed-forward) are exposed without policy controls governing their use.
