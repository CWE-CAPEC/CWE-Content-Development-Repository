SUBMISSION TYPE: Hardware, Software

NAME: Improper Verification of Compiled Quantum Circuit Integrity

DESCRIPTION:

Between submission and execution, a quantum circuit passes through a
classical compiler/transpiler, calibration pipeline, scheduler, and result
path. This weakness exists when the system executes a compiled or
transpiled quantum circuit without verifying that the executed circuit
faithfully corresponds to the circuit the user submitted. An adversary who
controls any stage of that classical pipeline can then silently modify the
circuit - perturbing rotation angles, deleting gates, inserting hidden
gates, remapping qubits, or substituting gates - so the computation that
runs differs from the one intended.

The primary technical impact is to Integrity: the executed computation
silently diverges from the user's intent and can be biased toward an
attacker-chosen result; a secondary impact is to Availability, through
degraded or incorrect results. Because each per-circuit modification can be
kept small and the quantum output is inherently noisy, the corruption is
hard to distinguish from ordinary device noise, and a persistent implant
can keep every deviation below single-run integrity tolerances while
accumulating an effect across many jobs. Variations include rotation-angle
perturbation, gate deletion, hidden-gate insertion, qubit remapping, and
gate substitution. The weakness is typically introduced at architecture and
design time, when the execution pipeline is specified without end-to-end
verification that the executed circuit matches the submitted circuit.
