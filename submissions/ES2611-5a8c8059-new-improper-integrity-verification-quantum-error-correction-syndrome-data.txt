SUBMISSION TYPE: Hardware, Software

NAME: Improper Integrity Verification of Quantum Error-Correction Syndrome Data

DESCRIPTION:

A quantum computing system that uses quantum error correction consumes a
stream of stabilizer syndrome data, which a classical decoder interprets to
compute error corrections. This weakness exists when the system does not
verify the integrity or authenticity of that syndrome data before the
decoder consumes it. An adversary positioned on the syndrome-extraction or
classical decoder path can then alter, replay, or suppress syndromes,
causing the decoder to apply incorrect corrections. The result is that
logical errors are induced even though the underlying physical qubit error
rate is unchanged, which makes the corruption difficult to attribute to an
attack rather than to ordinary device noise.

The primary technical impact is to Integrity - the logical computation is
silently corrupted and can be steered toward an attacker-chosen outcome -
and to Availability, through an elevated logical error rate that denies
correct results. Variations include targeted syndrome injection (altering
specific bits to steer a chosen logical outcome), random syndrome injection
(raising the logical error rate), syndrome replay (feeding stale syndromes
to mask a live error), and syndrome suppression (dropping syndromes so real
errors go uncorrected). The weakness is typically introduced at
architecture and design time, when the control and decoder pipeline is
specified without end-to-end verification of syndrome integrity and
authenticity.
