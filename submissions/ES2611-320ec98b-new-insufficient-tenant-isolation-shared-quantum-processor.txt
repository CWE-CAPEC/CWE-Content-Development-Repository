SUBMISSION TYPE: Hardware

NAME: Insufficient Tenant Isolation on a Shared Quantum Processor

DESCRIPTION:

The product operates a quantum processor in a multi-tenant mode but does
not sufficiently isolate co-resident tenants, so one tenant's circuit can
corrupt, or leak state to, another tenant through crosstalk between
physically coupled qubits or through incomplete qubit reset between tenant
jobs.

On a shared quantum processor, multiple tenants' circuits are scheduled
onto qubits of the same physical device. Qubits that are physically coupled
exhibit crosstalk: operations on one qubit perturb its neighbors. If the
scheduler places an aggressor tenant on qubits coupled to a victim
tenant's, the aggressor's gate activity can bias or degrade the victim's
results without any access to the victim's circuit. The same physical
adjacency, combined with incomplete reset between jobs, can leak residual
state from one tenant to the next. Because the effect manifests as elevated
error on the victim's output, it is difficult to distinguish from ordinary
device noise, and the victim has no signal that another tenant caused it.

Example: Tenants A and B are co-scheduled on adjacent qubits. Tenant A
repeatedly drives high-rate operations on its qubits; the induced crosstalk
shifts Tenant B's measured expectation values beyond B's tolerance,
silently corrupting B's computation while A's own results appear nominal.
Consequences span integrity (victim computation silently biased),
confidentiality (residual state leaking across tenants), and availability
(degraded results). The weakness is introduced at architecture and design
time when multi-tenant scheduling is built without isolation buffers or
crosstalk-aware qubit placement.
