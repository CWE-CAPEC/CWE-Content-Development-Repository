NAME: Insufficient Constraints in Cryptographic Proof Relations

DESCRIPTION:

The product relies on a cryptographic proof to establish a computation or
predicate, but the relation used for proof verification omits or
incorrectly encodes a required security condition. Consequently, a proof
that satisfies the implemented relation can be accepted even though the
intended computation or predicate has not been established.

Proof-based software separates the production of supporting values, called
the witness, from the constraints that establish which values are
acceptable. An honest witness generator may calculate every value
correctly, while the enforced constraints permit other values that violate
the intended security property. Successful tests using the honest generator
therefore do not establish that the proof relation adequately constrains
the computation.

The defining mistake is that the enforced relation establishes a weaker
claim than the application relies upon. This can arise when a condition is
checked only during witness generation, when required bounds or
relationships are omitted, when program logic is incorrectly translated
into constraints, or when circuit components are combined without enforcing
their shared assumptions.

Affected contexts include zero-knowledge proof circuits, virtual machines
that prove program execution, recursive proof aggregation,
privacy-preserving credential checks, and outsourced computation. The
weakness is not specific to one programming language, proof protocol, or
blockchain.

For example, a helper may compute a result without the proof relation
adequately binding that result to the claimed calculation. A credential
circuit may establish possession of a valid credential while omitting a
predicate required for eligibility. An aggregation circuit may verify
component proofs individually without enforcing a required relationship
between their public states. These are illustrative manifestations of the
same constraint-enforcement failure.

Potential consequences include acceptance of incorrect computational
claims, unauthorized state changes, or acceptance of ineligible
credentials. The impact depends on how the application uses the verified
claim.

This weakness concerns missing or incorrect constraints in the proved
relation. It excludes defects in an underlying cryptographic protocol or
verification algorithm that accepts a proof violating that relation. It
also excludes witness-generation errors that only prevent valid proofs, and
internal values whose freedom does not weaken a required security property.
Multiple valid witnesses alone do not establish underconstraint.

Prevention requires specifying the intended security claim and ensuring
that the enforced constraints establish it, including relevant domain
restrictions and composition invariants. Correcting only an untrusted
witness generator does not repair an inadequate proof relation. Changes to
the relation must also be reflected in the verification artifacts accepted
by relying parties.

Underconstrained circuits are an established research topic. This proposal
seeks a dedicated CWE representation of their recurring failure mechanism,
rather than claiming first discovery. Existing validation and verification
CWEs provide broad or partial coverage; the proposed entry would supply
proof-specific boundaries, detection guidance, and remediation.
