SUBMISSION TYPE: Software

NAME: Reliance on Preservation of Authentication Material Placed Outside the Protocol's Declared Message Boundary

DESCRIPTION:

The product attaches authentication material to a message outside the
extent declared by the protocol's framing, and relies on that material
reaching the receiver unchanged. A protocol-aware intermediary that
reconstructs the message from its parsed representation does not reproduce
material outside that extent, so the receiver acts on a message that was
authenticated in intent but is unauthenticated in fact.

Relays, bridges, gateways and brokers commonly rebuild a message from the
fields they parsed rather than forwarding the octets they received. This is
normal, specification-conformant behaviour, and is frequently the
intermediary's stated purpose, for example when translating between
protocol versions or transport domains. When authentication material sits
outside the region the framing accounts for -- appended past a length
field, carried in a metadata field the intermediary is not obliged to
forward, or otherwise outside the parsed structure -- it is not part of
what the intermediary reconstructs, and therefore does not appear in what
it emits.

The distinguishing property is that no attacker participates in the loss.
The sender behaves as designed, the intermediary behaves as specified, and
the receiver accepts a well-formed message. An adversary is required only
afterwards, to exploit the resulting unauthenticated channel, and the
capability required is whatever injection on that network segment already
required before the authentication was deployed. Deploying the
authentication therefore does not reduce the feasibility of that injection
at all, although a risk assessment will normally record it as if it did.

The failure is silent in both directions. The sender observes an
authenticated message leaving. The receiver observes a valid message
arriving, and cannot distinguish "no authenticator was attached" from "one
was attached and was not carried". Nothing on the path necessarily logs
anything. A system that authenticates correctly in a two-endpoint test
therefore stops authenticating the moment an intermediary is introduced,
which is the production topology for most deployments of this kind. This
makes the weakness resistant to testing that assumes tampering produces an
error.

Technical impact is to Integrity, Non-Repudiation and Access Control. The
receiver acts on commands whose origin cannot be established, and any
record that assumed the authentication was present loses its evidentiary
value. Where the receiver actuates, the impact is the actuation itself.

The weakness is introduced in architecture and design, typically when
authentication is retrofitted onto a protocol whose framing was not
designed to carry it, because the authenticator does not fit the field
available. Migration to post-quantum signatures makes this systematic
rather than occasional: their sizes exceed the fixed authenticator fields
of most embedded and control protocols by two to three orders of magnitude,
so an implementer adding them is structurally pushed to place them outside
the counted region.

The weakness is present when all four of the following hold, and absent if
any one fails: (1) messages are delimited by a length field or by a schema;
(2) authentication is placed outside what that delimitation accounts for;
(3) something on the path parses and reconstructs rather than forwarding
octets; (4) the receiver performs no integrity check over the raw octets it
received. The fourth condition is what makes the loss silent, and it is
frequently the cheapest one to remove.

Note that this differs from weaknesses describing an adversary who modifies
a message in transit. Here the intermediary is not compromised, not
misconfigured, and not in violation of any specification. That difference
changes both the attack feasibility rating and the effective mitigation:
hardening the channel against interference does not address it, and neither
does strengthening the authentication algorithm, since the algorithm is
never reached.
